Where was Treaty 1 signed?

Treaty 1 was signed on August 3, 1871, at Lower Fort Garry in southern Manitoba. It is also know as the Stone Fort Treaty, it was the first of the numbered treaties in Canada.

Who was involved

The Treaty was signed between the Canadian government and the Anishinaabe and the Swampy Cree people. Today, there are seven First Nations that represent the treaty 1:

  • Brokenhead Ojibway Nation
  • Long Plain First Nation
  • Peguis First Nation
  • Roseau River Anishinabe First Nation
  • Sagkeeng First Nation
  • Sandy Bay Ojibway First Nation
  • Swan Lake First Nation

What makes treaty 1 unique?

Treaty 1 is diffrent because it includes outside promises. These were promises that were not written down on the treaty. They included farming tools, animals, and schools. First Nations leaders had to protest for many years before the government officially recognized these promises in 1875.

Treaty 1 to Today

Treaty 1 is still important today. One example is Naawi-Oodena, which means Center of the Heart and Community. It is being built on the former Kapyong Barracks land in Winnipeg. It supports housing, jobs, and businesses for Treaty 1 First Nations.

Treaty 1 Promises

The Canadian government made many promises to the First Nations in Treaty 1.

  • Each family would receive land to live on
  • Money payments would be given every year
  • Farming tools and animals would be provided
  • Schools would be built on reserve land

Some promises were not written down, These are called outside promises.
